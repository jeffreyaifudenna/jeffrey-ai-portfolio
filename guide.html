How to get real work out of AI

Seven habits, no jargon. About a five-minute read.

Most people try an AI tool once, get a vague answer, and decide it's overhyped. The tool is rarely the problem. The way you ask is. These are the habits that made the biggest difference when I started building things with AI.

1. Start with the job, not the tool

Write down what you want finished, in one sentence: "a one-page summary of this report for my manager", not "something with AI". A clear job tells you whether AI is the right help, and gives you a way to judge the result.

2. Give it the context you'd give a new coworker

AI can't see your situation unless you describe it. Say who the work is for, what it's for, what good looks like and what to avoid. Paste in the real material, such as notes, an earlier draft or the email you're replying to. Two minutes of context beats ten rounds of fixing a generic answer.

Try this: "I'm writing for [who]. The goal is [goal]. Here's what I have: [paste]. Keep it under [length] and avoid [thing]."

3. Ask for a draft, then steer

Treat the first answer as a rough draft, not a verdict. Reply with what to change: "shorter", "less formal", "start with the main point", "the second paragraph is wrong, because...". Specific feedback gets a better second try. Repeating the same request does not.

4. Check what matters

AI writes confidently even when it is wrong. Names, numbers, dates, quotes and links are the places mistakes hide. Open the links, confirm the figures, and ask it to show its sources or reasoning. The more it matters if you're wrong, the more you verify. For anything public, I check every link before it goes out.

5. Match the tool to the job

Different tools are good at different things. A chat assistant is good for writing, thinking something through and analyzing documents. Image tools are good for visuals. Video tools such as Viggle are good for short clips. Try the same task in two tools once and see which result you'd actually use.

6. Keep a prompt you trust

When a prompt works, save it somewhere you'll find it again, in a note or a file. Change only the parts that vary. Over time you build a small toolkit, and the repeat tasks take a minute instead of twenty.

7. Automate only after it works by hand

Once a task gives good results every time, you can schedule it to run on its own, which is how the Daily AI News on this site gets published each morning. Do it by hand until you trust it, then automate it. Automating a shaky process just makes mistakes faster.

Where to go next

Pick one small task you did this week, like an email, a summary or a to-do list, and run it through these habits.
